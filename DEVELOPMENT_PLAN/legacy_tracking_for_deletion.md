## 1. Register contract

Exactly one reader-facing legacy register is permitted, at this path. A second current, historical,
superseded, archived, or completed-row ledger is prohibited. Git history retains deleted explanations.
A row may be removed only after its typed transition, independently qualified reintroduction cases, and
complete owning-phase gate pass under the accepted certification baseline.

The canonical-file structural seam checks one regular, non-executable UTF-8 register and absence of the
forbidden archive basename. Ordinary documentation checks may inspect metadata, headings, links, and the
disclosed basename-substring and case-folded forbidden-name diagnostics. Neither seam interprets table cells,
IDs, owners, counts, or ordering as executable legacy semantics.

Authored Haskell owns the closed `LegacyId` inventory, total owner map, observation and closure bindings,
required reintroduction identities, and lifecycle transitions. Every raw source finding must join to that
inventory in both directions. Missing, duplicate, wrongly owned, stale, unavailable, or unbound findings
must refuse at typed loci. Declaring a constructor, analyzer, or negative-case key does not establish closure.

This register also hosts the audit map of every plan re-sequence, as
[`development_plan_phase_model.md` §E](development_plan_phase_model.md#e-one-canonical-phase-model)
requires, under [§7](#7-the-audit-map). A receipt-bearing reset names a `productGap` that is one row here;
a reset with no product gap is refused while any `LTD-DSL-*` row is open
([DL-0008](../documents/decision_log.md#dl-0008--plan-re-sequence-into-a-vertical-slice)).

The tables preserve the existing identities and owning obligations. Their rationale explains why a binding
exists; it does not assert that every historical path still exists. Editing this document cannot add,
remove, reassign, or satisfy an executable binding.

Certification generation 2 requires the content-addressed verifier, protected custody, authenticated
receipts, and closure-based compatibility specified by
[gate integrity §M.0](development_plan_gate_integrity.md#m0-accepted-baseline-and-certification-generation) and
[§M.6](development_plan_gate_integrity.md#m6-candidate-evidence-and-gate-pass). The repair bindings named
below remain unimplemented by this documentation change. Earlier lifecycle observations, public diagnostic
facades, packaged implementation paths, and printed success claims cannot substitute for running the actual
authority path against the protected accepted baseline.

A closure candidate must meet all of these requirements:

1. Capture the complete version-controlled snapshot, including path, kind, mode, and bytes, and bind the
   acquired source identity to the executing analyzer and its protected acceptance baseline.
2. Report every matching path or semantic locus from complete discovery. Caller-provided counts and
   hash-shaped values cannot replace observations or authenticate their producer.
3. Satisfy the independently specified closure rule through the actual production authority path, without
   deriving behavior or expectations from this Markdown.
4. Apply each required reintroduction to an isolated acquired subject. Observe the exact assigned failing
   locus set and passing unaffected controls. No-op checking, constant success, empty discovery, skipped
   negatives, unrelated exceptions, and fabricated attribution must fail qualification.
5. Establish current compatibility for the immediate predecessor and every required dependency. Historical
   passes remain historical facts; unknown impact, missing dependencies, other due bindings, and unresolved
   required obligations prevent current acceptance.
6. Publish retrievable authenticated evidence, pass the complete owning-phase gate, and let the human's
   `accept` apply its authorized lifecycle and status transitions. The documentation check then reconciles
   the explanation with the Haskell-owned transition.

The target `amoebius validate legacy --id <ID>` command decodes and dispatches a typed Haskell identity.
Before `BOOTSTRAP_HANDOFF` passes, validation invokes the source-bound Haskell verifier directly. Python
neither calculates nor interprets a closure verdict. A row update introduces no status transition of its own;
the human's `accept` of the owning phase is the only transition.

[tracker](README.md#phase-overview) alone reports the current frontier.

1. Phase 0 builds the validator and the governance surface and closes `LTD-VAL-001` through `LTD-VAL-004`,
   `LTD-VAL-006`, and `LTD-KRN-001` through `LTD-KRN-003`.
2. Phase 1 owns authenticated toolchain acquisition, probe closure, and top-level vendor closure. Phase 2
   owns complete source classification, the `pb` pin, metadata, and capability naming.
3. Phase 3, the typed spine, closes `LTD-DSL-001`, `LTD-DSL-002`, `LTD-DSL-009`, `LTD-SRC-002`,
   `LTD-SRC-003`, `LTD-DOC-001`, and `LTD-VAL-005` for the spine.

5. Phase 9, `DSL_BARRIER`, requires every `LTD-SRC-*` and `LTD-DSL-*` query to be zero, decides
   `LTD-LIB-001`, and re-runs the union corpus through the shipped binary.
6. Phase 50, `BOOTSTRAP_HANDOFF`, owns `LTD-VAL-007`, `LTD-VAL-008`, and `LTD-HELPER-001`. Phase 51,
   `HOST_ENSURE`, owns the host rows. Phase 52, `FIRST_HARDWARE`, may begin only after its predecessor chain
   is accepted.
7. Phases 55, 56, 70, 72, 91, and 93 retain their later rows. `LTD-LIB-002` has no numbered owner until the
   proof-assistant track is promoted.

A missing path, edited explanation, implemented constructor, or historical pass cannot close a binding.
The Haskell-owned closure, independent reintroduction cases, current dependency compatibility, authenticated
evidence, the complete gate, and the human's `accept` must agree.
