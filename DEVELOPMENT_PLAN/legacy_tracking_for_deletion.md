## 1. Register contract

Exactly one reader-facing legacy register is permitted, at this path. A second current, historical,
superseded, archived, or completed-row ledger is prohibited. Git history retains deleted explanations.
A row may be removed only after its typed transition, independently qualified reintroduction cases, and
complete owning-phase gate pass under the accepted certification baseline.

The canonical-file structural seam checks one regular, non-executable UTF-8 register and absence of the
forbidden archive basename. Ordinary documentation checks may inspect metadata, headings, links, and the
disclosed basename-substring and case-folded forbidden-name diagnostics. Neither seam interprets table cells,
IDs, owners, counts, or ordering as executable legacy semantics.

Authored Haskell owns the closed `LegacyId` inventory, total owner map, observation and closure bindings,
required reintroduction identities, and lifecycle transitions. Every raw source finding must join to that
inventory in both directions. Missing, duplicate, wrongly owned, stale, unavailable, or unbound findings
must refuse at typed loci. Declaring a constructor, analyzer, or negative-case key does not establish closure.

The tables preserve the existing identities and owning obligations. Their rationale explains why a binding
exists; it does not assert that every historical path still exists. The August 2026 file counts and intermediate
component results are historical observations, not a current inventory, lifecycle definition, or acceptance
baseline. Editing this document cannot add, remove, reassign, or satisfy an executable binding.

The 2026-09-08 certification reset requires a new accepted Haskell baseline, protected verifier custody,
authenticated receipts, and dependency-aware compatibility. Their canonical requirements belong to
[gate integrity §M.0](development_plan_gate_integrity.md#m0-accepted-baseline-and-certification-generation) and
[§M.6](development_plan_gate_integrity.md#m6-candidate-evidence-and-gate-pass).
The new Haskell repair bindings and generation enforcement remain unimplemented by this documentation
refactor. Earlier lifecycle observations, public diagnostic facades, packaged implementation paths, and printed
success claims cannot substitute for running the actual authority path against the protected accepted baseline.

The typed `LTD-VAL-007` and `LTD-VAL-008` identities now exist in Haskell. Their existence corrects the former
missing-identity explanation; it supplies no evidence of new-generation acceptance. The same distinction
applies to every implemented analyzer and previously reported closure.

A closure candidate must meet all of these requirements:

1. Capture the complete version-controlled snapshot, including path, kind, mode, and bytes, and bind the
   acquired source identity to the executing analyzer and its protected acceptance baseline.
2. Report every matching path or semantic locus from complete discovery. Caller-provided counts and
   hash-shaped values cannot replace observations or authenticate their producer.
3. Satisfy the independently specified closure rule through the actual production authority path, without
   deriving behavior or expectations from this Markdown.
4. Apply each required reintroduction to an isolated acquired subject. Observe the exact assigned failing
   locus set and passing unaffected controls. No-op checking, constant success, empty discovery, skipped
   negatives, unrelated exceptions, and fabricated attribution must fail qualification.
5. Establish current compatibility for the immediate predecessor and every required dependency. Historical
   passes remain historical facts; unknown impact, missing dependencies, other due bindings, and unresolved
   required obligations prevent current acceptance.
6. Publish retrievable authenticated evidence, pass the complete owning-phase gate, and apply only its
   authorized lifecycle and status transitions. The documentation check then reconciles the explanation with
   the Haskell-owned transition.

The target `amoebius validate legacy --id <ID>` command decodes and dispatches a typed Haskell identity.
Before `BOOTSTRAP_HANDOFF` passes, validation invokes the source-bound Haskell authority directly through
its admitted bootstrap input. Python neither calculates nor interprets a closure verdict. Complete qualified
passes permit automatic progression; a row update introduces no routine human approval step.

## 5. Closure order

Compiled bindings close in their typed owning-phase order. The plan retains Phases 0–95 without renumbering.
The sequence below explains dependencies; it does not define executable ordering or a second status tracker.
The [tracker](README.md#phase-overview) alone reports the current frontier.

1. Phase 0 establishes its finite accepted bootstrap seed and qualification scope without requiring the
   universal Phase-49 harness. It records later-owned obligations explicitly; its scoped Python admission
   cannot retire the Phase-2 binding or authorize use of `pb` as validation transport.
2. Phase 1 owns authenticated toolchain acquisition, probe closure, and top-level vendor closure. Phase 2
   owns complete source classification, static bounded-bootstrap closure, metadata, and capability naming.
3. Each semantic capability qualifies its actual mechanism and exact mutation attribution at first use.
   Phases 11–14 must close the identified formal-semantics regressions within their original scope.
4. Phases 25 and 26 own schema and wire projections. Phase 27 removes behavioral Markdown/TSV authority.
5. Phases 40–42 must close the checked-plan/browser integration gaps within their original scope.
   Phases 46 and 47 remove the remaining authored generated programs, fixtures, tools, and mutants.
6. Phase 49, `DSL_BARRIER`, requires every source-migration binding, including bounded `pb`, to be zero.
   It composes compatible predecessor results through the real hardware-free pipeline and independently
   qualifies the complete universal gate protocol.
7. Phase 50, `BOOTSTRAP_HANDOFF`, validates the already source-bounded runtime handoff and owns
   `LTD-VAL-007`/`008`. Its Haskell supervisor starts directly and observes `pb` as the child subject.
   Phase 51, `HOST_ENSURE`, then validates the Haskell host kernel against fake boundaries without hardware.
8. Phase 52, `FIRST_HARDWARE`, may begin only after its predecessor chain is accepted. Its live Linux
   obligations require independent effect observation and exact attribution. Specialized hosts, images,
   clusters, providers, and seed removal retain their later numeric owners.

A missing path, edited explanation, implemented constructor, or historical pass cannot close a binding.
The Haskell-owned closure, independent reintroduction cases, current dependency compatibility, authenticated
evidence, and complete gate must agree. Unknown impact fails closed; unaffected accepted dependency closures
may be reused under the compatibility rule without rerunning every phase after every edit.
